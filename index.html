The Mastery of Ramen and Noodles

Noodles are the undisputed backbone of Japanese soul food, and here in the UK, they have found a massive and dedicated following. When you dive into the options, you will quickly notice the sheer variety of noodle bases—from thick, chewy udon to the thin, delicate ramen noodles. The ramen bowls are an experience in themselves, featuring rich, slow-cooked broths that have been simmered for hours to extract maximum umami flavour. A popular choice is the Shirodashi pork ramen, which balances the saltiness of the broth with tender, slow-roasted pork belly, seasonal greens, and a perfectly soft-boiled tea-stained egg.

If you prefer your noodles wok-fried rather than in a soup, the teppanyaki dishes are exceptional. Yaki Soba, a true classic, combines soba noodles with egg, peppers, beansprouts, and your choice of chicken, prawn, or tofu, all brought together with a sweet and savoury sauce, garnished with crispy fried onions and pickled ginger. These dishes are cooked fast and hot on a flat griddle, ensuring the vegetables retain their crunch while the noodles absorb the smoky flavours of the wok. Iconic Curries and Donburi Bowls

No discussion of modern casual dining in the UK is complete without mentioning the iconic Katsu Curry. It is arguably the dish that introduced millions of British diners to the joys of Japanese-style curry. The Chicken Katsu features succulent chicken breast coated in crispy panko breadcrumbs, served alongside sticky white rice and a generous pouring of aromatic, slightly sweet, and mildly spiced curry sauce. It’s a comforting, satisfying meal that hits the spot every time, regardless of the famously unpredictable British weather.

Beyond the curries, Donburi bowls offer a different kind of comfort. These are large bowls of steamed rice topped with stir-fried meats or vegetables. The Teriyaki Donburi, for example, is a masterclass in balancing sweet and savoury flavours. Tender strips of chicken or beef are glazed in a sticky teriyaki sauce, served over rice with shredded carrots, pea shoots, and a sprinkle of sesame seeds. The beauty of these bowls lies in their simplicity and the high quality of the ingredients used, making them a staple for lunch breaks and relaxed evening dinners alike.

Championing Vegan and Vegetarian Dining

In recent years, the culinary landscape of the United Kingdom has seen a massive shift towards plant-based eating, and modern menus have had to adapt quickly. What is truly commendable is how these dietary preferences are not just accommodated, but celebrated with incredibly creative dishes. The plant-based menu is extensive and thoughtful, ensuring that vegans do not miss out on the signature bold flavours.

The Vegatsu, a vegan take on the famous Katsu Curry, uses seitan coated in panko breadcrumbs. It perfectly mimics the texture and satisfying crunch of the original, paired with the exact same legendary curry sauce. Additionally, dishes like the Kare Burosu ramen—a warming bowl of shichimi-coated silken tofu and grilled mixed mushrooms in a spicy vegetable broth—demonstrate that meat-free options can be just as complex and deeply savoury. Sides such as the Bang Bang Cauliflower, featuring crispy wok-fried cauliflower coated in a firecracker sauce, have become legendary in their own right, proving that plant-based eating can be thrilling and full of fire.

Essential Sides and Shareables

The concept of sharing food is deeply ingrained in Asian dining culture, and this translates beautifully to the UK restaurant scene. The side dishes are designed to be ordered for the table, allowing everyone to mix and match flavours. Gyoza—those delightful Japanese dumplings—are an absolute must. Whether you choose the steamed, grilled, or deep-fried varieties, filled with duck, pork, or vegetables, they offer a perfect bite-sized burst of flavour, especially when dipped in a tangy soy and vinegar sauce.

Other notable sides include the chilli squid, which is crispy, spicy, and incredibly addictive, and the edamame beans, either served simply with salt or tossed in a spicy chilli garlic sauce. Ordering a selection of these sides alongside your main course elevates the entire dining experience, bringing a dynamic and interactive element to the meal.

Fresh Juices and Decadent Desserts

To balance the robust and spicy flavours of the main dishes, the drinks menu focuses heavily on freshly squeezed juices. These juices, blending ingredients like apple, mint, celery, and lime, are pressed to order and provide a refreshing, palate-cleansing contrast to the rich curries and broths.

For those with a sweet tooth, the desserts round off the meal perfectly. The White Chocolate and Ginger Cheesecake offers a fantastic fusion of traditional Western dessert mechanics with Eastern flavour profiles, while the selection of mochi ice cream provides a lighter, fun way to finish your meal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best dishes on the menu?

Some of the most popular dishes include the Chicken Katsu Curry, Yaki Soba, and the Shirodashi Pork Ramen. These staples have been fan favourites in the UK for years due to their comforting and consistent flavours.

Are there good vegan options available?

Absolutely! The UK menu offers an extensive vegan selection, including the Vegatsu (a vegan version of the Katsu Curry), Yasai Yaki Soba, and various plant-based sides like Bang Bang Cauliflower. Over 50% of the modern menu is plant-based.

How much does a typical meal cost in the UK?

A standard main course generally ranges between £12.00 and £16.00. Sides are usually around £5.00 to £7.00, making it an accessible casual dining option for most budgets.

Does the restaurant offer gluten-free dishes?

Yes, they provide a specific non-gluten menu which includes rice noodle dishes, specific curries made without wheat-based soy sauce, and modified salads to accommodate those with gluten intolerances.

Can I take away or order for delivery?

Yes, most branches across the United Kingdom offer both click-and-collect takeaway services and home delivery via major food delivery platforms.
